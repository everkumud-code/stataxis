Thirty channels, one event: how to read a live compare screen

When something big is on air, put up to thirty channels side by side. Here is what each column means and what it does not.

StatAxis · 2026-10-11 · 5 min read

When a result, a budget or a final is on air, the newsroom question is the same: how is everyone covering this, and who is winning the room? The live compare screen answers it, refreshed every fifteen seconds.

Choosing channels

Pick up to thirty, or type a few words describing the event. StatAxis takes every channel live right now whose stream title contains one of them. The rest are listed as not live.

The columns

  • Live now. This minute’s viewers.
  • 5 min. Change against five minutes ago.
  • Trend. The last half hour, as a line.
  • Peak and average. Over 30 minutes to 3 hours; the average counts only live minutes.
  • Live for. How long the stream has been on.
  • STX. The channel’s index and confidence: general strength, not tonight.
  • Tone. Positive, negative or mixed, from the words in the title.

About tone

Tone is indicative. It reads how a channel frames the event from its title, not how its audience feels; live chat is not read. Each row shows how many words were recognised, so a thin reading is not mistaken for a strong one.

Read the 5-minute column first, STX second, tone last. Audience moves show what is happening; tone shows how it is told.

A picture to post

One click turns the top ten into a branded card: channel, audience now, STX.

The rule

Compare channels on audience that is moving and framing that is visible, not on one number at the loudest minute.
