Thirty channels, one event: how to read a live compare screen

When something big is on air, put up to thirty channels side by side. Here is what each column means and what it does not.

StatAxis · 2026-10-11 · 5 min read

On the night of a result, a budget or a final, the question in every newsroom and every agency is the same: how is everyone else covering this, and who is winning the room? The live compare screen answers it on one page, refreshed every fifteen seconds.

Choosing the channels

You can pick up to thirty channels yourself, or type a few words that describe the event, such as budget or result. StatAxis then takes every channel that is live right now with a stream whose title contains one of your words. A channel that is not live is listed as not live. Nothing is guessed.

The columns

  • Live now. The channel's viewers this minute: the latest sample of each of its live streams, added together.
  • 5 min. How that compares with five minutes ago. A channel climbing while the room falls is the story.
  • Trend. A small line of the last half hour, so a spike and a climb do not look alike.
  • Peak and average. Over the window you choose, from thirty minutes to three hours. The average counts only the minutes the channel was live.
  • Live for. How long the current stream has been on. A channel that has just joined will not have built its audience yet.
  • STX. The channel's STX index for the period you choose, with its confidence. It tells you how strong the channel is in general, not how it is doing tonight.
  • Tone. Positive, negative or mixed, read from the words in the stream's title.

About the tone column

Tone is indicative, and the screen says so. It is read from the words in the title with plain positive and negative word lists. It tells you how the channel is framing the event, not how its audience feels; StatAxis cannot read live chat. Each row shows how many of the title's words were recognised, so a thin reading is not mistaken for a strong one. Across all channels, the headline tone is weighted by audience.

Reading tip: sort your eye by the 5-minute column first, the STX column second, the tone column last. Audience moves tell you what is happening; tone tells you how it is being told.

A picture to post

One click turns the top ten into a branded picture card: channel, audience now, STX. It carries the StatAxis mark and a plain statement of what was measured.

The rule

Compare channels on audience that is moving and framing that is visible. Do not compare them on a single number, least of all one taken at the loudest minute.
