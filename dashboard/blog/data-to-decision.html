From a live stream to a decision: the eight steps behind every StatAxis finding

A finding is not an opinion. It is a rule applied to measured numbers. Here is the path every one takes.

StatAxis · 2026-10-11 · 5 min read

Most dashboards stop at the chart. StatAxis keeps going until there is a sentence a person can act on, and then it shows its working. The path has eight steps, and none of them is optional.

1. Collect

Public YouTube data is read every few minutes, and live streams every thirty seconds. Each measurement keeps its timestamp.

2. Verify

Integrity checks look for inconsistencies in the stored data. The authenticity radar looks for unusual viewership. Flags are shown, not hidden.

3. Measure

Raw counts become measures: weekly views by group, prime-time audience by hour, the STX index for channels, the IFX Score for creators.

4. Find

Rules over the measures produce findings. This channel took 36% of the views of new videos in its group. This hour is the biggest of the evening. This genre led. Each finding carries the numbers behind it and a confidence: high when the data is dense, low when it is thin.

A finding says what the data shows. It never says why it happened.

5. Brief

The same findings are written for five readers. A media company sees its share and its hours. A buyer sees where the audience is and which channels to avoid. A campaign sees when attention peaks and how much of it is on politics. A creator sees their pillars. An audience member sees what India is watching tonight. Same facts, different advice.

6. Report

The brief becomes a branded PDF: findings first, then the tables, then how it was worked out. Each copy is made for one reader, carries the StatAxis mark and cannot be read by a script.

7. Act

Every finding links to the tool where you can act on it: the planner, the audience view, the radar, the report builder.

8. Learn

When a reviewer clears or confirms a flagged pattern, that decision is kept, and it changes what the next report says. The system gets more careful as people use it.

Where the data is too thin to state a finding, the brief says so in plain words. Nothing is estimated to fill the page.

The rule

If a number cannot show its working, it is not a finding. It is a rumour with a chart.
