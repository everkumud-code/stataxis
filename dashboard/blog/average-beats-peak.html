Why the average audience beats the peak when you buy media

A peak is a story. An average is a price. How to tell them apart before you sign a rate card.

StatAxis · 2026-10-10 · 5 min read

Every media plan has a number on its first page, and nine times out of ten it is a peak. The biggest night. The best minute. The screenshot that went round the office. It is a lovely number. It is also the wrong one to pay for.

A peak is a moment. An average is a habit.

A peak is the highest single minute in a window. It tells you what a channel can draw, and it is dominated by whatever happened that night: a result, a verdict, a breaking story. An average is the audience a spot will usually meet. When you buy a slot for next Tuesday, you are buying Tuesday, not the best night of the year.

Price a spot on the average. Treat the peak as upside.

How StatAxis counts

For each day in your range, StatAxis looks at the hours you choose, in Indian time, minute by minute. In every minute a channel's audience is the latest sample of each of its live streams, added together; a sample older than three minutes is not used. The average is taken over the minutes the channel was live. The peak is the highest of those minutes. Across days, the daily averages are averaged over the days the channel was live in that window. Nothing is interpolated, and a slot with nobody live is left out rather than counted as zero.

Three more numbers that matter more than the peak

  • Steadiness. How alike the days are. A channel that draws the same crowd every evening is easier to plan than one that swings.
  • Trend. Whether the second half of your window is above or below the first. A channel on the way down is dearer than its average says.
  • Days seen. How many of the days in your range the channel was actually live in that window, and the confidence that follows from it.

From audience to price

Type what a spot costs on each channel. The planner divides your rate by the average audience in thousands. That is the cost per 1,000 live viewers. Compare it with the middle of the channels you priced and you have a value index: 100 is the middle, higher is more audience for the rupee.

One honest caution. Live viewers are the people watching at the same moment per insertion. They are not unique reach, and audiences of different channels overlap by an amount no public data can show. The planner tells you so on every screen.

The rule

Ask for the average, the steadiness and the days seen. If a plan leads with a peak and cannot give you the other three, it is selling you a story.
