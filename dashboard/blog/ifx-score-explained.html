Impact, Followership, eXposure: how we score people who publish

The IFX Score ranks creators and independent journalists on three pillars, and says how sure it is.

StatAxis · 2026-10-11 · 6 min read

A channel can have a million subscribers and a median video a few thousand people watch. Another has fifty thousand who turn up every time. A follower count cannot tell them apart. The IFX Score can.

Three pillars, twelve parts

Impact (35%): how strongly the content lands. Depth of reaction (likes plus five times the comments, per view), conversation (comments per 1,000 views) and staying power (videos older than ten days still gaining views).

Followership (35%): the audience behind the creator. Size, loyalty (a mature upload’s median views as a share of subscribers), growth once three days of history exist, and live turnout (peak viewers at the creator’s own live streams, against subscribers) for those who go live.

eXposure (30%): how far and how often they are seen. Reach per upload, regular publishing, steady results, hit rate (how often an upload reaches the typical peer upload) and fresh uploads (how recently they published).

Ranked against peers

Each part is ranked within the comparison, not on a fixed scale. A part that cannot be measured is left out and the rest re-weighted.

Confidence, built in

Every score carries a confidence from uploads, subscriber data and days of history. Missing evidence pulls the shown score toward 50, so three uploads cannot earn a confident 90. Low-confidence scores are tagged preliminary.

A score that is never unsure is not measuring anything.

A profile in plain words

Steady, Rising, Hit-driven, or Impact-led, Follower-led, Exposure-led, Balanced. It also names the better format, Shorts or full videos, when both can be compared.

What it does not measure

Saves, shares and conversions are not public. Other platforms are not seen; the score is YouTube only for now. An open high-risk radar flag takes 10 points; a confirmed one withholds the score.

The rule

Use the score to decide where to look. Then read the three bars.
