Impact, Followership, eXposure: how we score people who publish

The IFX Score ranks creators and independent journalists on three pillars, and says how sure it is.

StatAxis · 2026-10-11 · 6 min read

Follower counts are the loudest number in creator marketing and the least useful. A channel can have a million subscribers and a median video that a few thousand people watch. Another can have fifty thousand and a loyal crowd that turns up every time. The count cannot tell them apart. The IFX Score can.

Three pillars, eight parts

Impact (35%) is how strongly the content lands. It is made of depth of reaction, likes plus five times the comments, divided by views, and staying power, how fast videos older than ten days keep gaining views.

Followership (35%) is the audience behind the creator: audience size, loyalty (the median views of a mature upload as a share of subscribers) and growth in subscribers, once there are three days of history to measure it.

eXposure (30%) is how far and how often the creator is seen: reach per upload (the median video), regular publishing (uploads per week and how even the gaps are) and steady results (how little views swing from one upload to the next).

Ranked against peers, never against a fixed scale

Each part is ranked against the other creators in the same comparison. A comedy channel is not marked against a news channel unless you ask it to be. A part that cannot be measured is left out, and the rest is re-weighted. The score is a weighted blend, out of 100.

A score that admits when it is unsure

Every score carries a confidence, built from the number of uploads, the presence of subscriber data and the days of history. The shown score is pulled toward 50 by what is missing: three uploads cannot earn a confident-looking 90. Low-confidence scores are tagged preliminary.

A score that is never unsure is not measuring anything.

A profile in plain words

Alongside the number, each creator gets a profile: Steady (results that hardly vary), Rising (the audience is growing quickly), Hit-driven (big reach, uneven results), or Impact-led, Follower-led or Exposure-led when one pillar clearly leads. It also names the best format, Shorts or full videos, when both have enough uploads to compare.

What it does not measure

Saves, shares and conversions are not public on YouTube, so they are not in the score. Exposure on other platforms is not seen: the IFX Score is YouTube only for now. And if the authenticity radar has an open high-risk flag on a channel the score loses 10 points; if an admin confirms a flag the score is withheld.

The rule

Use the score to decide where to look and who to call. Then read the three bars. They say more than the number.
