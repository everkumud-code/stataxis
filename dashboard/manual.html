STATAXIS · USER MANUAL

How to use StatAxis

This manual explains what StatAxis shows, where every number comes from, how the STX Index and the IFX Score are worked out, how to read the screens without being misled, and what to ask of any audience-data tool. It is written for people who decide things with the numbers: programme heads, advertisers and agencies, campaign teams, researchers, creators and journalists.

One sentence to remember. StatAxis measures what people do on YouTube, minute by minute, from YouTube's own official data; it shows how sure it is of every score; and it leaves a gap instead of inventing a number.

1 · What StatAxis is

StatAxis is an audience-measurement workspace for news, business and creator channels on YouTube. It reads live audiences and uploads of the channels it tracks, turns them into ranked, comparable figures, and explains each figure: what it is, when it was read, how many readings stand behind it and what it cannot tell you.

For media companiesSee where you stand against your market, which streams carried a spike, and how your prime time did against what was expected.
For advertisers and agenciesCompare channels on live audience, hours watched and cost per 1,000 live viewers before you spend.
For campaigns and researchersCheck what people are watching, the tone of titles, and run your own polls and surveys.
For creators and journalistsGet an IFX Score that judges your content, not only the size of your following.

What StatAxis is not

  • It is not television ratings. The figures are measured on YouTube. A channel's TV audience is not in them.
  • It does not prove anything about a channel's honesty. A flag on the authenticity radar is an unusual pattern in public numbers, nothing more.
  • It does not guess. If something cannot be measured it is shown as “—”, never as zero and never filled in.

  1. Request access. Use Apply for access. You are told when your login is ready. Until then the demo shows the real workspace on invented “Sample” channels, so you can learn the screens without risk.

4 · Where the data comes from

  • Source. The official YouTube Data API only. Nothing is scraped, and nothing is bought from anyone else.
  • Live streams. Each live stream is read about every 75 seconds. The interval is set by the quota YouTube allows and is shown on the status page.
  • Uploads and channel totals. Read in a pass about every 107 minutes. New live streams of the tracked channels are looked for about every 30 minutes in between, and each is followed as a secondary stream from the moment it is found.
  • Which channels. The list of tracked channels is the list shown on the Market and Operations screens. A channel that is switched off is not collected.
  • Facebook, Instagram, X. Only through each platform's own official access, or from sheets a channel supplies or StatAxis staff enter. Each figure carries the time it was read.
  • Time. Every reading is stored with the moment it was taken; screens show Indian time.

6 · The STX Index

The STX Index is one transparent score for a channel: how strongly its audience is moving, compared with the window before and with the channels it is compared with. It is a score for live channels and it uses YouTube data only. It is built from seven signals, each on a scale of 0 to 100 where 50 is neutral.

SignalWeightHow it is worked out

How the score is put together

STX = sum(signal × weight) ÷ sum(weights of the signals that exist) Confidence = sum(weights of the signals that exist) ÷ 1.00 × 100 Shown score = 50 + (STX − 50) × Confidence ÷ 100 Under 50% confidence the score is marked “preliminary”.

Two things follow, and they are the heart of the method. A signal that cannot be worked out is left out, and the rest are rescaled, so a missing number never drags a score down by being zero. And the shown score is pulled toward 50 in proportion to how little data there is, so thin data cannot produce a confident-looking score. A channel's STX is withheld until it has a second reading to compare with.

Worked example

A channel's signals this week: audience 60, growth 55, momentum 48, acceleration 50, consistency 70; it ranks 4th of the channels compared, so competition = 100 ÷ 4 = 25; anomaly 52.

All seven exist: 60×0.30 + 55×0.20 + 48×0.15 + 50×0.10 + 70×0.10 + 25×0.10 + 52×0.05 = 18 + 11 + 7.2 + 5 + 7 + 2.5 + 2.6 = 53.3 Confidence 100% Shown 53.3 Acceleration and anomaly are missing (weights 0.10 and 0.05): available weight = 0.85; (18 + 11 + 7.2 + 7 + 2.5) = 45.7; STX = 45.7 ÷ 0.85 = 53.8 Confidence = 85%; Shown = 50 + (53.8 − 50) × 0.85 = 53.2

How to read an STX Index

  • Around 50: the channel is about where it was in the window before and in the middle of the channels compared.
  • Above 50: its audience and views are rising against the earlier window, it is gaining on its peers, or both. Below 50: the opposite.
  • Read the parts, not only the total. The same 55 can mean “steady and high-ranked” or “a one-off jump”. The STX screen lists every part and how many points each added.
  • Check confidence first. A score under 50% confidence is preliminary. Treat it as a pointer to look closer, not as a verdict.
  • Compare like with like. The score depends on the window and on the channels compared. Compare scores that were worked out over the same window and market.
  • It does not say why. It shows that something moved, with the evidence; it does not infer causes.

7 · The IFX Score

The IFX Score (Impact · Followership · eXposure) is for people who publish videos and Shorts instead of going live: creators, independent journalists, commentators. A creator has no live audience to measure, so the score is built from what a creator does have in public. It is ranked against the other creators in the same comparison, not against a fixed scale.

PillarPartWeight

How it is worked out

  1. Each of the sixteen parts is measured for every creator in the comparison and turned into a rank position (a percentile) among them.
  2. The parts are combined with the weights above into the three pillars, and the pillars into one score (Impact 35% · Followership 35% · eXposure 30%).
  3. A part that cannot be measured (for example, live turnout for a creator who never went live) is left out and the others re-weighted.
  4. The score is pulled toward 50 by (1 − confidence), so thin data cannot give a confident-looking score. Every row shows its confidence, a plain-words profile and the reason behind a high or low score.
  5. A creator with open high-risk unusual-viewership flags loses 10 points; one whose flag an administrator confirmed is not scored.

A fair start for small creators. Four parts do not depend on how big the creator already is: the best upload against audience size, reaching beyond own subscribers, momentum (newer uploads against older) and the early pull of new uploads. A small creator who makes things people want can score well on them.

What the parts mean in plain words

  • Depth of reaction: likes and comments per view. Conversation: comments per 1,000 views. Old videos still earn: how fast videos older than 10 days keep gaining views.
  • Audience size: subscribers. Loyalty: views of a typical mature upload per subscriber. Growth: subscriber change (needs three or more days of history). Live turnout: the creator's own live streams against subscribers.
  • Reach per upload: typical views. Regular publishing: uploads per week and how even the gaps are. Steady results: how little views vary from upload to upload. Hit rate: how often an upload beats the typical peer upload. Fresh uploads: how recently the creator published.

How to read an IFX Score

  • It is a ranking inside a group: change the group and the scores change. Use it to compare creators you actually compete with.
  • Look at the pillars: high Impact with low Followership is a creator whose content lands but whose audience is still small; high Followership with low Impact is a big audience that reacts little.
  • Not measured: saves, shares and conversions (YouTube does not publish them for other people's videos) and exposure on other platforms. This score is YouTube only, and says so.

8 · Methodology in short

The full, versioned methodology, with a dated changelog of every change to a formula, weight, window or threshold, is on the Methodology page. It is read from the same settings the calculations use, so it cannot drift from them. In short:

  • A regular grid, no guessing. Live figures are read on a one-minute grid. Each live stream counts with its latest reading for up to 180 seconds; after that it is not counted. Nothing is interpolated.
  • Windows. Last hour, 3 hours and 6 hours end now. “Today” runs from midnight Indian time. Prime time is the hourly slots 17:00–23:00. Each window is compared with the one of equal length before it.
  • Forecasts. The expected prime-time audience is the median of the same slot over up to seven earlier days, written down at 06:00 IST and scored afterwards. A hit is within 20%. The track record is shown on the Forecasts screen.
  • Authenticity radar. It flags unusual patterns in public numbers, with the evidence. A flag is not proof and an administrator's decision is recorded beside it.
  • Social media. Official access only; reach and impressions are not public on any platform, so the engagement rate says how many people reacted, not how many saw a post. STX and IFX use YouTube data only; social figures sit beside them, not inside them.
  • Limits, stated plainly. Figures are measured on YouTube, not on television. A flag is a pattern, not a finding.

9 · How to read the data

A five-step habit

  1. Check the frame. Which window, which language or market, which feed? A number means little until you know all three.
  2. Read the size and the shape. Average says what is typical; peak says what is possible. A peak far above the average is a spiky channel (an event or a breaking story). A flat line is a channel whose audience does not move with events.
  3. Look for gaps. On a chart a broken line means no live stream then. It is not zero. “Time live” shows how much of the window the average stands on.
  4. Split the feeds. If All is much higher than Primary, an event stream added the audience. If All equals Primary, the channel had nothing else live.
  5. Read the evidence line and the confidence. How many readings? How much of the score is backed by data? Then decide how hard to lean on it.

Worked reading

“Channel A: average 42,000, peak 91,000, time live 100%.” A live audience that is usually 42,000 and reached 91,000: an event night, not a normal one. Open Spikes and movement to see which stream carried the rise.

“Channel B: STX 61, confidence 40% (preliminary).” Only 40% of the weights could be worked out, so the score is pulled strongly toward 50 and the true score may be quite different. Look at which signals are missing before using it.

“Channel C: All 120,000, Primary 70,000, Secondary 50,000.” Its 24×7 stream has 70,000 and a parallel event stream added 50,000. If you are buying the 24×7 slot, use Primary.

What not to conclude

  • Do not read a ranking as “the best channel”. It is the best on the figure shown, for that window and market.
  • Do not compare a single peak minute across days without the average.
  • Do not read a flag as an accusation, or a gap as zero viewers.
  • Do not treat YouTube figures as TV ratings.

14 · FAQ

What is StatAxis in one line?

An audience-measurement workspace for YouTube news, business and creator channels, with transparent scores (STX and IFX), live figures read minute by minute, and an honest statement of how sure each number is.

Are these television ratings?

No. Everything is measured on YouTube. A channel's television audience is not in these figures.

Where does the data come from?

The official YouTube Data API, and for Facebook, Instagram and X only each platform's own official access or sheets a channel supplies. Nothing is scraped.

How often is it updated?

Each live stream is read about every 75 seconds; uploads and channel totals about every 107 minutes; new live streams are looked for about every 30 minutes. The exact interval is on the status page.

What is the difference between concurrent viewers and views?

Concurrent viewers are the people watching a live stream right now. Views are a running total of how many times a video or stream has been viewed; it only grows.

What is the difference between average and peak?

Average is the typical live audience over the minutes a channel was live; peak is the highest single minute. A big gap means a spiky, event-driven audience.

What are Primary, Secondary and All feeds?

Primary is a channel's long-running main stream (live for at least 30 days). Secondary is every other stream it has live, usually for an event. All is both together.

Why is there no Primary feed for a channel?

No stream of that channel has been live long enough to count as primary. Its viewers are then counted as secondary so that All is never understated.

Why does a chart line stop and start?

A gap means no live stream at that time. It is deliberately not drawn as zero.

What does an STX Index of 50 mean?

Neutral: the channel is about where it was in the window before and in the middle of the channels compared. Above 50 is rising or gaining on peers; below 50 is the opposite.

Why does my STX show “preliminary” or “—”?

“Preliminary” means less than half of the signals' weight could be worked out, so the shown score is pulled strongly toward 50. “—” means there is not yet a second reading to compare with.

How is the STX Index calculated?

Seven signals (audience 30%, growth 20%, momentum 15%, acceleration 10%, consistency 10%, competition 10%, anomaly or event 5%) are each put on a 0–100 scale. The score is the weighted average of the signals that exist, then pulled toward 50 by how much of the weight is missing. Section 6 has the formula, a worked example and a calculator.

How is the IFX Score different from the STX Index?

STX is for live channels and leans on live audiences. IFX is for creators who publish videos and Shorts: sixteen parts under Impact, Followership and eXposure, each ranked against the other creators in the same comparison.

Why does my IFX Score change when I change the comparison group?

Each part is ranked against the others in the group. A different group is a different yardstick, so the same creator can rank differently.

Why are my numbers different from YouTube Studio or another tool?

Studio shows your own channel's analytics with its own definitions and delays. StatAxis reads public figures on a published one-minute grid and states its definitions. Different definitions and read times give different numbers; compare the definitions first.

Why are Shorts left out of rankings?

They are a different kind of viewing. Shorts are collected and can be viewed but are not counted in rankings, STX or analysis.

What time zone is used?

Indian time (IST) everywhere, including “Today”, prime time and every download.

What does the authenticity radar flag mean?

An unusual pattern in public numbers, shown with the evidence. It is not proof of anything, and an administrator's decision is recorded beside it.

How good are the forecasts?

They are written down at 06:00 IST before the slot and scored afterwards; a hit is within 20%. The hit rate is on the Forecasts screen, so you can judge them.

Can I download the data?

Yes, if your administrator ticked it: Excel files with the raw numbers, branded PDFs and picture cards, and e-mailed reports. Each carries the time it was made.

I cannot see a screen that this manual describes.

Screens depend on your package and on the privileges your administrator ticked. Ask your administrator, or use Apply for access to request a package.

How do I get access, and where can I try it first?

Use Apply for access. The demo shows the real workspace on invented sample channels, with nothing real in it.

Is Instagram, Facebook or X data included in the STX Index or IFX Score?

No. Both use YouTube data only. Social figures are shown beside them, not inside them, and come only from official access.
